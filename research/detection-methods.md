# Lightweight AI-text detection for Slop Shame

Research note, 2026-10-19. Constraint: an MIT Chrome extension that scores LinkedIn posts locally, with no network calls and a budget under 1 ms per 300-word post. Today the engine is phrase dictionaries plus sentence-length uniformity, type-token ratio, em-dash density, and rule-of-three. This note asks what else is worth adding.

Verdicts mean:

- **adopt** — fits the budget, the license, and a public shame button.
- **maybe** — useful offline (calibration, or a later opt-in), not on the feed hot path.
- **skip** — too slow, too big, license-incompatible, or too biased to shame someone with.

Every number below is taken from the linked source. None of these methods is accurate enough, on a 50–400 word post, to treat a score as proof that a person used a model.

The score function already in the tree saturates raw evidence into 0–100:

`evidence = raw * (0.55 + 0.45 / sqrt(wordCount / 100))`, then `score = min(100, round(100 * (1 - exp(-evidence / 32))))`.

See `src/lib/engine/score.ts`. The recipe in section 6 is defined in that raw-point scale.

## 2. Stylometric and information-theoretic features

Short-text evidence first, because a LinkedIn post is often under 400 words and sometimes under 50.

**Length is the dominant limit.** On 25,561 paired academic texts, a logistic regression on 45 stylometric features has AUC 0.817 (73.9% accuracy) at 25 words, 0.863 (78.2%) at 50, 0.902 (82.3%) at 100, and 0.940 (86.8%) at 300 words. Catching 95% of AI rewrites means falsely flagging 68% of human texts at 25 words and 34% at 300 words. A classifier trained on full texts and applied to 25-word cuts flags 70% of human texts, because short texts of either authorship have mechanically high type-token ratio. Sentence-level variation carries almost no signal under 50 words, where texts average fewer than three sentences. Word-level features hold up better. ([TextPulse, 2026](https://textpulse.ai/research/textpulse-ai-detection-text-length-2026.pdf); scores and code at [Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.22050346](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22050346).) OpenAI's own 2023 classifier, withdrawn on 20 July 2023, was "very unreliable" below 1,000 characters, caught 26% of AI text, and false-flagged 9% of human text ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/new-ai-classifier-for-indicating-ai-written-text/)).

**Burstiness (sentence-length coefficient of variation).** Human prose varies in sentence length; autoregressive samples are smoother. A 2025 comparison of LLaMA and a diffusion model defines burstiness as std/mean of sentence lengths and reports that diffusion text mimics human perplexity and burstiness, so a single metric fails ([arXiv:2507.10475](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.10475)). GPTZero popularised perplexity plus burstiness; a commercial critique argues the pair fails when the generator is closed (no token probabilities), when text is short, and when the domain is technical ([Pangram](https://www.pangram.com/blog/why-perplexity-and-burstiness-fail-to-detect-ai)). On tweets, stylometry alone is real but modest: an XGBoost on stylometric features scores 0.771 accuracy on single in-house tweets (N=1) and 0.847 on TweepFake, behind fine-tuned RoBERTa (0.807 in-house at N=1) and well behind the same stylometry model once a timeline has 20 tweets (0.958) ([Kumarage et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.03697), Table 2). **Verdict: adopt, gated.** Compute CV only when there are at least four sentences. It may add a few points. It must not shame a post by itself.

**Type-token ratio.** Lexical diversity is one of the metrics in the diffusion study above, and it is in every heuristic package in section 1. The length study shows why a raw TTR is unsafe: short human text looks "diverse" for mechanical reasons, and a long-text threshold then calls it AI ([TextPulse, 2026](https://textpulse.ai/research/textpulse-ai-detection-text-length-2026.pdf)). **Verdict: skip as a positive AI feature.** If it is kept, use a length-normalized form (MATTR or root TTR) and only as a damper or a calibration covariate, never as points toward shame.

**Function words.** On 100 Japanese public comments versus 350 texts from seven LLMs, function-word unigrams, POS bigrams, and phrase patterns separate the classes; a random forest on the three together reaches 99.8% accuracy (99.0% human recall) ([Zaitsu et al., PLOS One, 2025](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0335369)). That is a small, single-genre, Japanese sample, and the authors show humans cannot make the same distinction. It is not a license to ship a Japanese function-word model, and it is not evidence about English LinkedIn posts. English function-word rates are still the classic authorship signal, and word n-grams (which include function words) carry signal but less than character n-grams in the fingerprint study below. **Verdict: maybe** as a small set of English rates (however, moreover, additionally, that said) inside the existing phrase pack. **skip** a full function-word distribution test until it is calibrated on short English social text, because formal and non-native prose lives in a different function-word region (section 5).

**Punctuation, em dashes, and the rule of three.** These tells move.

- Population web text, not per-document detection: in a Pew sample of 490,000 English pages from Common Crawl, em dashes per 10,000 words go from 5.79 (January 2023) to 11.19 (January 2026), and a fixed "AI vocabulary" list goes from 11.94 to 26.02 ([Pew chart](https://www.pewresearch.org/chart/key-words-and-punctuation-favored-by-ai-has-spiked-since-the-release-of-chatgpt/)). A rise on the web does not identify one post.
- Per-model em dashes are not a universal excess. Across twelve models, unconstrained em-dash rates run from 0.0 per 1,000 words (Llama) to 10.62 (GPT-4.1). A human essay sample (57,232 words) averages 3.23 per 1,000 words, with a range of 0.33–17.12. GPT-5.4 drops to 1.43. Instructing models to avoid markdown removes headers, bullets, and bold; em dashes sometimes remain and sometimes do not ([arXiv:2603.27006](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.27006)).
- *The Economist* (July 2026), comparing 14 model variants to its own prose plus other newspapers and novels, over 55,940 sentences and 1.2 million words: only Claude uses more em dashes than the human writers; ChatGPT uses markedly fewer. Stronger tells in that corpus are fewer commas, semicolons, and parentheses, longer sentences joined by "and", Latinate vocabulary, and the constructions "not X but Y", "not only but also", and the rule of three ([SMH reprint](https://www.smh.com.au/technology/how-to-spot-if-something-has-been-written-by-ai-20260807-p60m9d.html), [Fast Company summary](https://www.fastcompany.com/91584243/how-to-identify-ai-generated-writing-viral-report-has-surprising-new-clues-economist), [archive of the article](https://archive.fo/i9UQP)).

**Verdict:** **adopt** rule-of-three, "not X but Y", and markdown leftovers (bullets, bold, headings inside a prose post) as positive evidence. **skip** em-dash density as a primary feature. A high em-dash rate can add at most a couple of points, and only when another positive signal has already fired. A low em-dash rate is not evidence of a human.

**Character n-grams.** On two single-generator slices, character 3–5-grams alone reach F1 0.96 / AUROC 0.99 (GPT-J, Deepfake) and F1 0.96 / AUROC 1.00 (Flan-T5, Outfox). Word n-grams are weaker (F1 0.77 and 0.86), which the authors attribute to topic leakage. The hypothesis is that character n-grams track BPE-like subword habits ([McGovern et al., arXiv:2405.14057](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14057)). On 10-sentence Wikipedia-style samples, tree models over StyloMetrix plus n-gram features reach binary accuracy from 0.79 to 1.0, including 0.98 for Wikipedia versus GPT-4, and up to 0.87 Matthews correlation in a 7-way task. SHAP points at encyclopaedic wording and grammatical standardisation, so part of the signal is genre ([Przystalski et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.00838v2)). **Verdict: maybe**, as an offline logistic regression on hashed character 4-grams (the weight vector can be kilobytes). **skip** shipping someone else's n-gram model. Retrain on short social text or the weights will learn "Wikipedia versus GPT-4", not "LinkedIn colleague versus Claude".

**Repetition of higher-order n-grams.** Unigram frequencies of modern samples follow human Zipf curves; higher-order repeats do not, in a corpus-level sense, for GPT-2 under ancestral and nucleus sampling ([Gallé et al., 2021](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2111.02878)). On RAID, human text is the least repetitive by Self-BLEU, and continuation models repeat more than chat models ([Dugan et al., ACL 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.07940v1), Figure 8). A within-post count of repeated sentence openers or repeated character 4-grams is O(length) and fits the budget. A corpus-level repeat detector does not: one post has too few repeats to estimate a distribution. **Verdict: adopt** a within-post repetition rate, capped and small. **skip** corpus-level repeat statistics.

**Compression (gzip / zlib / NCD).**

- Gzip-KNN classifies by normalized compression distance to labeled neighbors. On HC3-derived English answers it reaches accuracy 0.83 with 100 training samples and saturates near 0.89, while logistic regression and XGBoost reach 0.91 and 0.94 once 5,000 samples are available. Inference takes about 5.9 seconds at 100 neighbors and 115.6 seconds at 2,000 ([Berchtold et al., ICNLSP 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.icnlsp-1.4.pdf)). **skip.** The accuracy is real and the latency is fatal.
- AIDetx builds a finite-context compressor per class and assigns the text to whichever model compresses it better. Reported accuracy is 97% on HC3 and over 99% on a second AI-vs-human set ([arXiv:2411.19869](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.19869)). Code is [GPL-3.0](https://github.com/AIDetx/AIDetx), which cannot be linked into an MIT extension. The method also needs reference corpora at runtime. **skip.**
- A PAN 2026 notebook stacks normalized compression distance from PPMd, zlib, bz2, and lzma with shallow n-gram rates (18 features) into XGBoost, arguing this avoids a language-model forward pass ([CEUR Vol-4283](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-4283/paper432.pdf)). The abstract does not give an accuracy. Multi-algorithm NCD against reference documents is the same latency class as Gzip-KNN. **skip** for the content script. A single `CompressionStream` ratio of one 300-word post is a weak repetitiveness proxy and is asynchronous, so it does not belong on the scoring path either.

**Binoculars, and why it cannot be approximated without a model.** Binoculars scores a string as observer log-perplexity divided by cross-perplexity between two models that share a tokenizer. The denominator estimates how surprising the *prompt* is, so a weird topic ("capybara") does not look human merely because both humans and models find it surprising. With Falcon-7B and Falcon-7B-Instruct, the paper reports over 90% detection of ChatGPT samples at a 0.01% false-positive rate, with no ChatGPT training data ([Hans et al., ICML 2024](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/hans24a.html); formula in [arXiv:2401.12070](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.12070v3)). On RAID, at a 5% false-positive rate, Binoculars averages 79.6% accuracy across generators and 79.4% on the Reddit domain; synonym swap drops it by 36.1 points ([Dugan et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.07940v1), Tables 4 and 13). Fast-DetectGPT, which replaces DetectGPT's perturbations with a closed-form curvature, averages 73.6% on the same RAID slice and 70.9% on Reddit, at a 340× speedup over DetectGPT on an A100 (about 4 minutes versus 22 hours for the paper's XSum batch) ([Bao et al., ICLR 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.05130v3); [code, MIT](https://github.com/baoguangsheng/fast-detect-gpt)). GLTR, the older token-rank histogram, averages 62.6% on that RAID slice ([Gehrmann et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.04043); [code, Apache-2.0](https://github.com/HendrikStrobelt/detecting-fake-text)).

A trigram table can fake the numerator. Nothing local fakes the cross-perplexity denominator, which is the part that handles hard prompts. Two 7B models are not a content-script feature. **Verdict: skip**, including "Binoculars-style" approximations. The design lesson to keep is the opposite of a heuristic: do not treat "this text is smooth" as evidence, because smoothness is what formal, technical, and non-native prose looks like.

Accuracy on a balanced academic corpus is the wrong objective ([RAID, Finding 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.07940v1)).

## 5. False positives, and what a shame button has to do about them

Detectors that look at smoothness punish the writers a shame button must not punish.

**Non-native English.** Seven widely used GPT detectors, evaluated on 91 TOEFL essays from a Chinese forum and 88 US eighth-grade essays, were near-perfect on the eighth-graders and wrong on the TOEFL set: average false-positive rate 61.22%. All seven agreed on 18 of 91 TOEFL essays (19.78%). Those unanimous misses had significantly lower perplexity. Prompting ChatGPT to "enhance the word choices" dropped the average false-positive rate to 11.77%. Simplifying the eighth-grade essays toward non-native wording raised the false-positive rate from 5.19% to 56.65% ([Liang et al., Patterns 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.02819v3); published version [doi:10.1016/j.patter.2023.100779](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.patter.2023.100779)). The mechanism is exactly low perplexity and low lexical variety. A feature that scores "uniform, simple, few contractions" is a feature that scores many non-native writers.

A 2025 audit of Copyleaks, ZeroGPT, Scribbr, and Quillbot (texts of about 400–500 words; ESL graduate writing from before 2021 versus published human text versus AI text) found the tools good at spotting fully AI text and disproportionately wrong on ESL writing ([IJTLE](https://doi.org/10.22161/ijtle.4.5.5)). On scholarly abstracts, GPTZero assigned higher AI probabilities to non-native authors than to native authors (means 44.61% vs 30.68%, Welch p = 0.036); ZeroGPT and DetectGPT did not show a significant gap in that study ([PMC12453642](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12453642/)). Byakugan's own README states the bias outright ([link](https://github.com/JayOfemi/byakugan)).

**Formal register.** The same smoothness is what edited, professional, and academic prose looks like. TextPulse's earlier stylometric classifier concentrated its false positives on formal human writers ([working paper](https://textpulse.ai/research/human-vs-ai-text-classification), cited from the length study). LinkedIn is a formal register. A burstiness or contraction penalty tuned on casual English will shame careful human posts, including posts by people writing in a second language at work.

**Edited and hybrid text.** Weber-Wulff et al. tested 14 detectors on 54 cases in 2023. All scored under 80% accuracy, only five over 70%, about 20% of AI text was called human, and obfuscation (light editing, translation, paraphrasing) pushed about 50% of AI text into the human bin ([doi:10.1007/s40979-023-00146-z](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40979-023-00146-z)). RAID shows the same brittleness at a fixed 5% false-positive rate: synonym swap costs Binoculars 36 points ([paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.07940v1)). A Spanish hybrid-text study finds none of Originality.ai, GPTZero, or Copyleaks reliable on mixed human/AI text ([doi:10.4114/inteletica.vol2iss4pp102-114](https://doi.org/10.4114/inteletica.vol2iss4pp102-114)). People paste a model draft and then edit it. The score has to live with that, not pretend to resolve it.

**Tells drift.** "Delve" and "tapestry" were real population spikes ([Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/chart/key-words-and-punctuation-favored-by-ai-has-spiked-since-the-release-of-chatgpt/)). By mid-2026 *The Economist*'s corpus finds those words fading, em dashes inverted for ChatGPT, and the remaining tells in punctuation scarcity, "and"-chained sentences, and rhetorical triplets ([archive.fo/i9UQP](https://archive.fo/i9UQP)). A phrase pack is a maintenance burden, not a fixed scientific instrument.

**What the better evaluations do, and what we should copy:**

1. Report true-positive rate at a low false-positive rate, not headline accuracy. Binoculars' paper argues AUC hides the only number that matters when a human is accused ([Hans et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.12070v1)). RAID fixes the operating point at 5% false positives for that reason.
2. Refuse a verdict when the text is short. OpenAI required 1,000 characters and still withdrew the tool ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/new-ai-classifier-for-indicating-ai-written-text/)). TextPulse's operating points become usable around 100 words and most of the AUC gain is in by about 150 ([length study](https://textpulse.ai/research/textpulse-ai-detection-text-length-2026.pdf)).
3. Do not use low perplexity, low TTR, missing contractions, or "too grammatical" as positive evidence. Those are the features Liang et al. show are doing the damage.
4. Prefer positive, quotable evidence (a phrase, a template, a markdown skeleton) over absence evidence. LAID and LinkedIn Detox already show the breakdown in the UI; the score should be explainable from the same signals ([LAID](https://github.com/oldeucryptoboi/linkedin-ai-detector/blob/master/README.md)).
5. Say so in the product. LinkedIn Detox's README is the right tone: the number will flag humans and miss machines, and it is not an accusation ([README](https://github.com/OdinMB/linkedin-detox)).

## 6. Recommended scoring recipe

Keep the phrase packs. They are the only signal that is both fast and specific to the thing this extension is mocking (LinkedIn template slop), and they can be quoted back to the reader. Add a few stylometric points that the short-text papers still support. Take points away for features that punish non-native and formal writers. Separate the displayed 0–100 from the decision to shame.

All caps below are in the raw points that `normalizedScore` already saturates. They are starting values, not fitted weights. Fit them on Unmasking the Imposters and the RAID Reddit slice, then freeze the shame threshold on a human formal/ESL holdout at ≤5% false positives.

### Positive evidence

| Feature | Raw cap | When it fires | Why this weight |
|---|---|---|---|
| Existing phrase packs (AI tells, LinkedIn templates, CTAs, emoji-bullet lines) | 24 | Unchanged matcher. Cap the category so a long post full of weak hits cannot run away. | Highest precision per millisecond. Brittle: retire dead tells ("delve", "tapestry") using the Economist/Pew shift, and keep structural phrases ("let's dive in", "I'm humbled to share"). |
| Discourse templates: "not X but Y" / "not only but also", rule of three, hook then list then CTA | 12 | Reuse the current rule-of-three and negation-pivot rules. | Still over-used relative to human news and fiction in the 2026 Economist corpus ([archive.fo/i9UQP](https://archive.fo/i9UQP)). |
| Markdown leaking into a prose post: bullet or numbered list of three or more items, bold labels, heading lines | 8 | Count lines, not a model. | Formatting instructions remove headers and bullets across providers; the em dash does not ([arXiv:2603.27006](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.27006)). Slopblock already treats emoji bullets and numbered outlines as local signals ([repo](https://github.com/JesseVent/slopblock-plus)). |
| Sentence-length CV | 8 | Only if there are at least 4 sentences. `cv = std/mean` of sentence word counts. Map `cv ≥ 0.55` to 0 points and `cv ≤ 0.25` to 8, linearly in between. | Burstiness is a real autoregressive tell ([arXiv:2507.10475](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.10475)) and is almost uninformative below three sentences ([TextPulse](https://textpulse.ai/research/textpulse-ai-detection-text-length-2026.pdf)). |
| Repeated sentence openers | 4 | The same first two words on at least 3 sentences, ignoring a short stoplist of "I" / "We" / "The". | Within-post repetition. Humans are the least Self-BLEU-repetitive class on RAID ([paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.07940v1)). |
| Em dash | 2 | Only if another positive feature has already scored, and only if the rate exceeds 8 per 100 words. | Human rates already span 0.33–17.12 per 1,000 words, and ChatGPT now uses fewer dashes than the Economist's human writers ([arXiv:2603.27006](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.27006), [Economist](https://archive.fo/i9UQP)). This is a tie-break, not a detector. |

Do not add points for: low type-token ratio, low "perplexity", missing contractions, few grammar errors, few personal pronouns, or a gzip ratio.

### Dampers

Subtract after the positive sum, and do not let the raw total go below 0.

| Damper | Up to | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Specificity: non-round numbers, named products, a concrete first-person incident | −6 | LAID uses this as a human signal ([README](https://github.com/oldeucryptoboi/linkedin-ai-detector/blob/master/README.md)). It is a damper, not proof of humanity. Models can fake specifics. |
| Informal markers: contractions, fragments, a typo, "idk" / "tbh" | −4 | Presence lowers the score. Absence adds nothing. Absence is the ESL and formal-writing trap ([Liang et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.02819v3)). |

### Combine

```
raw = phrases + discourse + markdown + rhythm + openers + emDash + dampers
score = normalizedScore(raw, wordCount)   // existing saturation
```

Worked examples at about 100 words, where the length multiplier is 1:

- Phrases at the cap (24) → score about 53. A bingo-card post reads "maybe", not "shame", until a second group fires.
- Phrases plus discourse (36) → about 68.
- Phrases plus discourse plus markdown (44) → about 75.
- Every cap stacked (58) → about 84.

That ceiling is intentional. A heuristic should have trouble claiming 90.

### Shame gate

The number on the badge can move with stylometry. Destroy and Shame cannot.

- Under 40 words, cap the displayed score at 35 and disable both buttons. Single-tweet stylometry is around 77% accuracy in the best small study found, on in-distribution GPT-2-era tweets, with no false-positive budget ([Kumarage et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.03697)).
- From 40 to 80 words, allow the badge, and allow Shame only when `score ≥ threshold` and at least one phrase or discourse signal fired.
- At 80 words and above, same rule. Rhythm and repetition may raise the badge. They may not, alone, cross into Shame.
- Set `threshold` on the human holdout so that at most 5% of formal and non-native posts are shamed. If the phrase packs cannot hit a useful true-positive rate at that false-positive rate, lower the caps. Do not lower the false-positive budget.

### What to build later, still not on the hot path

A logistic regression on the features above, plus hashed character 4-grams, trained on Unmasking the Imposters and RAID-Reddit and evaluated at 5% false positives. Ship only the weights (kilobytes). Keep the shame gate in front of it. Revisit MiniLM prototype matching only if paraphrase of the phrase packs becomes the main miss, and only as an opt-in worker: the int8 model is 23 MB ([file tree](https://huggingface.co/Xenova/all-MiniLM-L6-v2/tree/main/onnx)).

### What not to build

A local Binoculars, a gzip nearest-neighbor, a DeBERTa ONNX, or an em-dash counter with LAID's 8% weight. The first three miss the latency and size budget. The fourth is aimed at the wrong model year, and it is the kind of rule that reads as an accusation while being wrong about ChatGPT specifically ([Economist, 2026](https://www.smh.com.au/technology/how-to-spot-if-something-has-been-written-by-ai-20260807-p60m9d.html)).
