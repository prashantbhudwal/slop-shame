# LinkedIn post data for a pre-ChatGPT human baseline

**Research date:** 2026-10-21
**Question:** Find human-written LinkedIn posts (or closely similar professional social posts) from before November 2022, under a licence that allows evaluation and, ideally, deriving numeric weights for an MIT-licensed browser extension. The post text itself would never be shipped.

Nothing found meets both conditions. The only large, dated, pre-November 2022 post text is scraped or page-captured, and the stated licences are share-alike, "© original authors", or absent. The one first-party grant (NeuML, Apache-2.0) is a 2024–2025 company export.

Datasets were not downloaded. Licences below are quoted from the primary record (dataset page, repository API, or dataset card). A mirror was not used as the licence source.

## How the verdicts were applied

- **NOT USABLE for weights** if the stated licence is CC BY-SA, NC, ND, share-alike (ODbL / DbCL, which Kaggle pairs with the Open Database License), no licence, or unclear, or if the same page contradicts a permissive badge.
- CC BY, CC0, Apache-2.0, and MIT are the stated-licence classes that could support weights. None of those classes is attached to a confirmed pre-November 2022 corpus whose uploader owns the posts.
- A CC0 or CC BY badge on text scraped from other people's LinkedIn accounts does not clear the authors' copyright. Those rows are marked NOT USABLE for weights even when the badge itself is permissive.
- "Research-only" means whether the stated licence allows private evaluation that does not redistribute the text. It does not mean the collection method complies with LinkedIn's terms.

## Notes on the top of the table

**Influencers and company pages (Kaggle, Shreya Sajal).** These are the only large structured text tables that are entirely from before November 2022. Both pages say "The data was scraped using BeautifulSoup and Selenium. Last updated on 15th Feb, 2021." That is automated collection of LinkedIn pages. Kaggle's own licence list maps "Database: Open Database, Contents: Database Contents" to `DbCL-1.0` and "Database: Open Database, Contents: © Original Authors" to `ODbL-1.0` ([Kaggle dataset docs](https://www.kaggle.com/docs/datasets); [CLI metadata](https://github.com/Kaggle/kaggle-cli/wiki/Dataset-Metadata/bc2684f533cd40afae28210d8f6e62b88d793d62)). DbCL 1.0 says "You must comply with the ODbL." ODbL is a share-alike database licence. Share-alike is in the reject list for weights in an MIT extension. The company-pages file is stricter: the contents stay "© Original Authors", so the post text is not licensed at all. Neither file has a human/AI label. A February 2021 cutoff means the text predates public ChatGPT. It does not mean every post was written without a ghostwriter or a template.

**OSF COVID PDFs.** This is the cleanest date proof. Brewer and Imes report examining every LinkedIn post from the 20 largest global B2B firms in February–October 2020 ([paper abstract](https://doi.org/10.1108/ccij-12-2022-0149)). The public OSF project stores that material as PDFs, not rows. There is no licence on the node. Public files without a licence are not a grant to fit weights or to treat the text as an evaluation corpus.

**Figshare Company A/B/C.** Same depositor, different project ("B2B Social Media Study", three midsized manufacturers). CC BY 4.0 is stated and would be the right licence family, but the posts are not shown to be the depositor's, the examination period is not dated, and the files are small spreadsheet supplements. Do not treat them as the 2020 COVID corpus.

**Wyrich and Bogner.** CC BY 4.0 is real on Zenodo record 10453832. The release is 98 posts found on 16 August 2023, with scraping scripts in `linkedin-post-scraping.zip`. The paper collected attributes (including character counts) rather than promising a full-text corpus, and the CSV is 46 KB. Useful as a methods note. Not a pre-2022 weight-fitting corpus.

**Yari 22K.** Largest open text file with a CC BY badge. The badge is contradicted on the same Zenodo page by "academic research and replication purposes only" and "All rights reserved." Cleaning deleted stopwords, so the strings are not natural posts. Dates span 2015 through March 2025 with no documented date column, so a pre-November 2022 slice cannot be cut from the documentation. No authorship label and no AI label.

**NeuML.** Best rights story, wrong era. The company exported its own analytics workbook ("Export the company posts from the analytics page") and marked the card `apache-2.0`. That is a first-party grant, not a scrape of strangers. All 258 posts are from the twelve months ending January 2025.

## Bottom line

No public dataset found on 2026-10-21 is confirmed pre-November 2022 human LinkedIn post text under a licence that allows numeric weights in an MIT extension.

The three closest pre-2022 deposits, in order, are Sajal's influencer CSV, Sajal's company-page CSV, and Brewer and Imes's 2020 firm PDFs. All three are NOT USABLE for weights: DbCL/ODbL share-alike, contents © original authors, and no licence. Private evaluation of the influencer file is the only one of the three whose stated database licence contemplates use without redistribution, and even that does not clear the posters' copyright or LinkedIn's terms.

The cleanest first-party grant is NeuML's Apache-2.0 export of its own company posts, and every one of those posts is from 2024–2025.
